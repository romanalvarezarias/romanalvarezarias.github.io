My dissertation develops tools to understand infinitely generated projective modules and how modules split into direct sums.

Publications

  • Torsion-free modules over commutative domains of Krull dimension one
    2026
    with D. Herbera and P. Příhoda · Revista Matemática Iberoamericana 42 (2026), no. 1, 123–186 · JournalarXiv:2406.14665
    Abstract

    For a one-dimensional domain R, we ask when the class of arbitrary direct sums of finitely generated torsion-free modules is closed under direct summands. For local R, this happens exactly when every indecomposable finitely generated torsion-free module has local endomorphism ring; if R is also noetherian, exactly when its normalization is local. For h-local R the property passes to all localizations at maximal ideals, and all but at most one of them have every finitely generated ideal 2-generated. The converse holds when R is integrally closed, noetherian semilocal, or noetherian with module-finite normalization. Over domains of finite character, countably generated modules in this class are classified by their genus.

  • Relatively big projective modules and their applications to direct sum decompositions
    2026
    with D. Herbera and P. Příhoda · Journal of Algebra and Its Applications, online ready, 55 pp. · JournalarXiv:2504.16568
    Abstract

    We study countably generated projective modules that are relatively big with respect to a trace ideal, a notion due to P. Příhoda that generalizes Bass's uniformly big projectives. We show that over right noetherian PI-rings, and over locally semiperfect torsion-free algebras over h-local domains, every countably generated projective module is relatively big. This applies to endomorphism rings of finitely generated torsion-free modules over h-local domains, and lets us characterize the h-local domains of Krull dimension one where direct summands of direct sums of copies of a finitely generated torsion-free module are again direct sums of finitely generated modules.

Ph.D. thesis

  • Relatively big projective modules, direct sum decompositions of modules, and representations of finite groups
    2026
    UAB and Charles University · advisors D. Herbera and P. Příhoda · Excellent cum laude · TDXUAB record
    Summary

    The thesis has three parts. First, it studies when direct sums of finitely generated torsion-free modules over a commutative domain are closed under direct summands, with complete answers for local and h-local domains of Krull dimension one. Second, it shows that every countably generated projective module is relatively big over several broad classes of rings, including right noetherian PI-rings and semiperfect rings, and derives decomposition results for endomorphism rings of torsion-free modules. Third, it extends this to locally semiperfect algebras with semisimple artinian ring of quotients, describing projective modules through dimension sequences and decomposition matrices, with group algebras of finite groups over Dedekind domains as the main example.
